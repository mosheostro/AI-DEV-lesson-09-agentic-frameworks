# IT Support Policy

Owner: IT
Effective date: 2026-06-01

## What a ticket needs

Tickets should include employee name, email, system, error text, urgency, business
impact, and safe screenshots (with any visible customer data redacted before
attaching). Missing information should be requested from the employee, not
invented or assumed by whoever is triaging the ticket — a guessed error message
sends the wrong person down the wrong path.

## Priority levels

- **High** — blocked customer-facing work, or an onboarding date at risk (a new
  hire who can't get into the systems they need on day one).
- **Medium** — the employee can work around the issue but it's slowing them down.
- **Low** — everything else, including feature requests and non-blocking questions.

## Response targets

High-priority tickets get a first response within 1 business hour and are worked
continuously until resolved or handed to Engineering. Medium-priority tickets get a
first response within 1 business day. Low-priority tickets get a first response
within 3 business days. These are response targets, not resolution guarantees —
some fixes take longer than the first response.

## Escalation

A ticket that has had no update in 2 business days at High priority, or 5 business
days at Medium, should be escalated to the IT team lead rather than left to age
silently in the queue.
